Privacy policy

Last updated 13 October 2026.

Google Calendar access

Calendar Alarms uses the Google Calendar API with the calendar.events.readonly permission. Google's permission allows read access to events across calendars, but the app queries only your primary calendar for timed events. It requests event IDs, status, and start date and time with time zone information. It does not request event titles, descriptions, locations, attendees, or other calendar content. It excludes all-day events. It does not create, change, or delete Google Calendar events.

Use and storage

The app checks Google Calendar about once a minute and keeps a rolling seven-day alarm schedule on your Windows computer. It stores event IDs, start times, alarm status, and dismissal history locally so alarms work during a temporary internet outage and dismissed alarms do not ring again. It uses these data only to schedule, ring, and dismiss your alarms. It also stores the OAuth client configuration and authorization tokens on your computer, encrypted with Windows Data Protection for your Windows account.

Sharing and retention

The app connects directly from your computer to Google's APIs. It does not send your calendar information or tokens to the developer, a separate server, advertisers, or analytics services. Local diagnostic logs record operational events and counts without event titles, event IDs, or tokens; logs older than 30 days are removed. The local schedule and dismissal history remain until you remove the application data. You can revoke Google access in your Google Account settings. To erase the local schedule and encrypted credentials, use the documented uninstall command with -RemoveData.

These public information pages are hosted by GitHub Pages. GitHub may process visitor information, including IP addresses, to deliver and secure the pages. The pages contain no analytics or tracking scripts. Google processes your information when the app uses the Google Calendar API, subject to Google's own privacy practices.
